# F063: compiler totality follow-up

Status: **Partially implemented, 2026-09-17; finding remains open.**
This handoff recorded work deferred under the Phase 1 prohibition on editing
`ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs`.
That prohibition has lifted, and proposals 1 (for the two named sites), 2 and 3
below have landed; the reachability audit that proposal 1 asks for in general
has not.
It is not an amendment to the architecture review or evidence of closure.

## What landed

- Three guest-facing sites are routed through the coder's error channel or the
  parser's, each with an explicit KIND.
  `({a = 1})` reports `ParseErrorKind::Syntax`, because it is a spec early
  error and the guest should see a catchable `SyntaxError`.
  The static-block fold reports `ParseErrorKind::Unsupported`, because it is a
  coverage gap.
  A multi-binding for-in/of head (`for (let x, y in {})`) is rejected in the
  parser: five shapes reached `code_assign`'s unreachable arm and panicked, and
  two of them sat in the committed test262 expectations as
  `skip:compiler-unimplemented:parse`.
- A reported error STOPS the pass.
  `Coder::report_kind` latches the error and unwinds with a private payload
  that `compile_parser` catches, which is XS's `longjmp` expressed in Rust.
  The first attempt latched and returned, guarded by a `poisoned()` flag
  consulted at one of the file's eighty-odd panic sites; the pass walked on and
  died at an unguarded one, losing the classification it had just recorded, so
  the diagnosis was worse than the `panic!` it replaced.
  `compile_budget.rs`'s `a_reported_early_error_stops_the_pass` gates it by
  cost, because a flag nothing reads cannot be tested.
- `SourceCompileError::Invariant` exists, the eval bridge maps it to an
  uncatchable `Halt::EngineInvariant("eval:compiler-invariant")` dropping the
  panic text rather than exposing it, and the production adapter
  (`ironhorse-runtime`) constructs it: its `catch_unwind` used to map a caught
  panic to `SourceCompileError::Unsupported`, i.e. to
  `Halt::NotImplemented("eval:compiler-unimplemented")`, which is the finding's
  claim at the live seam.
- `compile_atoms_budgeted_firewalled` and its UTF-16 sibling are the one place
  a panic becomes `CompileError::Invariant`, so an embedder does not hand-roll
  a catcher and guess what a panic means.
  Every adapter in the tree, the `thixotrope-ironhorse-worker` binary included,
  compiles through them.
- The test262 harness files a caught panic as a `Fail` named
  `compiler-panicked:<phase>` (`Category::IronhorseFailure`) instead of sharing
  `compiler-unimplemented:<phase>` with real coverage gaps.
  Filing it as a named *skip* was the first attempt and was still wrong: an
  unclassified skip label falls to `Category::Infrastructure`, which the report
  renders as "not Ironhorse gaps", so the fault left the Ironhorse column
  altogether.
- `corpus_compiler_totality.rs` compiles every one of test262's 53,575 sources
  and requires that none of them panics.
  It currently passes; with the for-in fix reverted it names the two cases
  that fail.
  `ironhorse-compile/tests/compiler_totality.rs` is the hand-written net beside
  it, and `ironhorse-vm/tests/compiler_fault_classification.rs` gates the VM
  seam: a fault and a coverage gap produce different halts, and the fault is
  uncatchable.
- `expectation_shards.rs`'s `committed_expectations_record_no_compiler_fault`
  refuses to let a compiler fault be written down as a baseline.

## What did not

The reachability audit. `coder.rs` still holds other `panic!` and
`unreachable!` sites and `scoper.rs` a wider `expect`/`unwrap` surface; most
are invariants over the parser's own output, but nothing has established that,
so the compiler is not total. The finding stays open on this.

What the corpus sweep establishes is narrower than the audit and is worth
stating exactly: no source in the pinned test262 corpus reaches any of those
sites. That is a strong empirical floor and it is not a proof — test262 is not
the set of all JavaScript, and a source outside it may still reach one.

## Proposed changes, including the frozen file

Proposals 1 (for the named sites), 2, 3 and 4 have landed; proposal 1's general
audit has not.

1. Audit guest-reachable coder/scoper failure sites and classify each as an early
   error, deliberate unsupported feature, or internal invariant.
   Reject cover-initialized names such as `({a = 1})` during grammar refinement;
   preserve valid destructuring forms.
   For deliberate folds, use the structured error channel and poison the pass on
   its first error so it cannot serialize partial output or trigger later panics.
   **Landed for the three named sites; the audit of the rest has not.**
2. In `interp.rs`, add a distinct compiler-invariant variant to `SourceCompileError`
   and handle it as an uncatchable `Halt::EngineInvariant`, with a stable diagnostic
   label rather than arbitrary panic text exposed to guests.
   The exact variant and label names are proposals, not an implemented API.
   Preserve the existing syntax, unsupported, meter, and heap mappings.
   **Landed** as `SourceCompileError::Invariant` and
   `Halt::EngineInvariant("eval:compiler-invariant")`.
3. Update compiler adapters, including the harness and production integration, to
   preserve that distinction through reporting and worker failure handling.
   A caught unexpected panic must not become `Unsupported` or a guest `SyntaxError`.
   Audit all public compilation entries and callers, not just the eval bridge.
   **Landed**, and the audit found the production adapter doing exactly that.
4. Retain unwind containment as defense in depth for internal bugs while removing
   guest-reachable panic control flow.
   Do not remove the `panic=abort` build rejection as part of this change.
   **Landed** as `compile_atoms_budgeted_firewalled`; the `panic=abort`
   rejection is untouched, and it is now load-bearing twice over, because the
   coder's own reported-error path unwinds.

Some compiler-local repairs can be made without changing `interp.rs`.
They would be partial work: the runtime classification obligation above would
remain unresolved.
This handoff does not claim that every component individually needs the frozen file.

## Machinery to preserve

[The meter](../ironhorse-compile/src/meter.rs) deliberately uses a private `Refused`
unwind payload and `catch_refusal` to implement sticky work refusal.
Do not convert that payload into an invariant error, swallow a callback panic, or
lose charges accrued before a refusal or compiler failure.
Preserve the accounting and retained-index contracts described in
[W4](../architecture-review/2026-09-06/W4-IMPLEMENTATION.md) and
[the performance tradeoffs](../architecture-review/2026-09-06/PERFORMANCE-TRADEOFFS.md).

## Acceptance tests for the follow-up

- Oracle-free compiler tests must obtain structured results, without unwinding,
  for static blocks containing lexicals and invalid cover-initialized names,
  including nested object forms; valid destructuring must still compile.
- Exercise every public entry, Script/Eval/Module goals, and budgeted/unbudgeted
  variants over the audited cases in debug and release builds.
- Use an injected `SourceCompiler` in `ironhorse-vm/tests/` to distinguish syntax,
  unsupported, invariant, meter, and heap outcomes at the real runtime bridge.
  A guest `try/catch` must not catch an invariant halt.
- Check sticky refusal and exact charged-work retention for failure paths, then
  run the existing compiler budget and corpus suites.

The first three are landed and passing; the fourth is landed for the budget and
corpus suites. What is not covered is the audit itself: every public entry over
*audited* cases presupposes an audit that has not happened.
