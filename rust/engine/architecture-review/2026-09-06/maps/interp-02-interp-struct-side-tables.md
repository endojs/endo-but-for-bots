## Purpose and responsibilities

This range is the *declaration* of the machine: the `Interp` struct (3651–4402, 165 fields:
42 `HashMap`s, 5 `HashSet`s, 105 fields whose type mentions `Slot`/`SlotIndex`/`ChunkOffset`/
`StaticStrings`), the frozen Intl data records (4420–4568), the `*Row`/`*Snapshot` types the
snapshot crate consumes (4596–4936), and the private activation/suspension records
(`CallerState`, `CatchJump`, `SavedFrame`, `SavedJump`, `GeneratorData`, `GenRunFrame`,
`AsyncData`, `AsyncRunFrame`, `AsyncGenerator*`, `ResumeStatus`, 4943–5176). It ends at
`impl Default for Interp` (5178) and the opening of the 38k-line `impl Interp` (5184).

Architecturally it is XS's `txMachine` plus every `txSlot` internal-slot union arm, flattened
into one struct: anything XS keeps *inside* a slot record as a union payload (array items,
Map entries, promise reactions, generator stacks, proxy target/handler, bound-function data,
Date value, RegExp program, Intl resolved options, …) ironhorse keeps in a Rust-side table
keyed by the owning `SlotIndex`. That decision is what makes the GC root set and the snapshot
ledger open-ended: the arena is structural, the side tables are not.

call the `*_snapshot`/`restore_*` verbs and consume the `pub` Row types; `machine.rs:1723,1759`
call `collect_garbage` after resume; `partial_collect` calls `free_pages`); the two
source-parsing nets (`tests/gc_visitation_registry.rs`, `sidetable.rs` tests) parse
`pub struct Interp {` textually; `pub slots`/`pub chunks` expose the arenas directly.

## Architectural observations

- **Monolith symptom, quantified.** 44 side tables, each requiring hand-parallel entries in up
  to nine places: `gc_roots`, `extra_edges`, `swept`, late-prune list, `ephemeron_edges`,
  `external_chunk_refs`, `each_side_table_ref_tail`, `free_pages`, the persist-gate holder
  walk, plus a `*_snapshot`/`restore_*` pair and two registries. The `Hooks` struct
  (43711–43755) re-borrows 39 fields to work around `&mut self` aliasing. The wave-6 misses
  (W6-1..W6-4, W6-19) are the empirical cost; the two textual registries are the compensating
  control. A per-table trait (`refs`, `chunk_refs`, `prune`, `snapshot`) would collapse the
  parallel lists into one impl per table — the natural refactor this struct is asking for.
- **GC is boundary-only by construction.** Nothing in `ironhorse-vm/src` calls the collector;
  the field docs ("no mid-run GC runs … the stage-2 GC roots contract", 4082–4084, 3029–3031)
  and the registry's `pending_new_target` note encode it. Consequences: (a) peak heap per crank
  is bounded only by the meter/step limit, not by collection; (b) Rust-local `SlotIndex`
  temporaries inside natives are never at risk — which is precisely why the collector cannot
  later be made allocation-triggered without a handle/rooting discipline; (c) the design's
  "XS's exact mark-and-sweep ported semantically" elides that XS collects under allocation
  pressure. No assertion (`debug_assert!(self.is_quiescent())`) pins the property.
- **Determinism.** Root/emitter ordering is handled well (sorted everywhere). `HashMap`
  iteration inside `extra_edges` (accessors/private tables, 43876–43902) and
  `ephemeron_edges` only affects mark worklist order, which is unobservable provided gc.rs's
  sweep (index order) and compaction (address order) are order-independent — claimed at
  43704–43706, owned by another region. The real determinism leak is NaN payload bits (finding 1).
- **Two f64s in side tables** (`dates`, `RegExpData.last_index`) are emitted as raw bits; both
  are produced only via `time_clip`/`f64::NAN` literals (39578–39581) or ToLength, so they are
  canonical — fine. The raw `Slot`s in `wrapper_data`, `private_values`, `BoundData`,
  `PromiseData`, `SavedFrame`, etc. are not.
- **Top-level lexical scope is per-crank.** `run` clears `locals`/`id_map` after a completed
  crank (11234–11238) and `BEGIN_*` hoists only `var`s to the global (11533–11546). So a
  script-level `let`/`const`/`class` from crank N is unresolvable in crank N+1 — consistent
  between continuous and resumed twins (so not a snapshot bug), but it is a multi-crank
  semantic that the single-script test262 parity bar cannot observe, and I could not confirm
  from this region whether XS's realm-global lexical environment behaves the same across
  `xsRunScript` calls. Flagged as unclear.
- **`is_quiescent` is narrower than the transient argument.** The ledger test only reconciles
  `.is_empty()` fields; `env`, `result`, `pending_new_target`, `dispatch_depth`,
  `resume_status`, `callback_return_depth`, `active_segment` are claimed default at the
  boundary but never asserted there.
- **Panics on the persistence path.** `function_state_snapshot` `.expect("every guest bytecode
  function owns a code segment")` (9232) and `intl_bound_functions_snapshot`'s
  `&self.functions[function]` (9526, 9537) index by invariant; `restore_intl_bound_functions`
  `.unwrap()` at 9579 is guarded by the `contains_key` match arm. All are internal-invariant
  panics (crashed crank, acceptable per doctrine) but they sit inside checkpoint verbs, where a
  panic loses the crank being checkpointed.
- **Unvalidated restore verbs.** `restore_wrapper_data` (9120), `restore_dates` (9205),
  `restore_arguments_brands` (9181), `restore_disposable_stacks` (9666) return `()` and insert
  any `u32` owner; validation is delegated wholesale to `image.rs`. Combined with `pub slots`/
  `pub chunks`, the crate boundary is wide.
- **Intl/Temporal is an oracle-blind consensus surface.** 3996–3999 says outright that any
  program touching Intl aborts in the oracle, so its metering (and results) are never
  compared; the design (§ test262) says "XS deliberately omits Intl; Ironhorse omits it
  identically", yet nine Intl tables, four Temporal tables and `INTL_DATA_VERSION` ship.
- **Per-call/per-`try` heap allocation.** `CallerState` owns three heap collections per call;
  `CatchJump` clones the frame's `HashMap` on every `try` entry (15671); `SavedJump` clones it
  per handler per suspension. XS pays none of this (frames are stack slots). Perf-envelope risk.
- **Memory sinks not under the meter.** `symbol_registry` (strong forever, spec-conformant),
  `code_segments` (every eval'd unit retained for the realm's life — documented 3753–3760),
  `symbol_names` (append-only). Allocation metering covers slots/chunks, not these Rust heaps.
- **`Rc` in `Interp`** (`source_compiler`, `code_segments`, `top_level_code`) makes the machine
  `!Send`, consistent with design resolved question 8.
- **API-boundary integer widths.** Exported rows are mostly `u32`/`u64`, but `SegmentsData`
  and `SegmentIteratorData` carry `usize`, narrowed with an unchecked `pos as u32`
  (`image.rs:2915`).

## Candidate findings

1. **No NaN canonicalization anywhere; snapshot codec preserves platform NaN payloads** —
   `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/value.rs:332` (and `interp.rs:40342–40343`,
   `ironhorse-snapshot/src/slot_codec.rs:189–203`). Claim: a guest NaN produced by arithmetic
   carries the host CPU's default-NaN bit pattern (sign bit differs between x86-64 SSE and
   AArch64), is observable through `Float64Array`/`DataView` byte views, and is written
   verbatim into `HEAP` and every Slot-bearing row (`BoundFunctionRow.this_arg/args`,
   `PrivateValueRow.value`, `PromiseRow.result`, `SavedFrameRow.*`), so identical inputs yield
   different snapshot bytes and different guest-visible bytes across architectures. Evidence:
   `pub fn number(n: f64) -> Slot { Slot::of(Kind::Number, Payload::Number(n)) }`;
   `// A signaling-NaN bit pattern must survive exactly, not collapse to a canonical NaN.`;
   `mxCanonicalNaN` appears only in `xs-oracle/build.rs:81`. Why it matters: the design's
   unconditional per-release determinism and the CAS content-addressing both assume
   bit-identical images; XS runs with `mxCanonicalNaN` for exactly this reason. Confidence:
   high that no canonicalization exists; the x86/ARM default-NaN sign divergence is well
   established but I did not run the two architectures here. [determinism, high]

2. **The boot-layout half of `SIGN` is a documentation convention, not a check** —
   `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:3738` (with `5377`, `format.rs:326–347`,
   `machine.rs:1660`). Claim: nothing derives the signature from `Interp::new`'s layout,
   `boot_slot_count` is never serialized or pinned by a test, so adding one intrinsic without
   bumping the hand string `"ironhorse-worker-v1"` lets adoption attach `functions`/`*_proto`
   boot metadata to unrelated image slots silently. Evidence: `boot_slot_count: u32,` (not in
   any `*_snapshot`); `interp.boot_slot_count = interp.slots.capacity();`; `pub struct
   Signature(pub String);`. Why it matters: silent-wrong class on the persistence path once
   containers outlive a build (the design decided they will). A serialized `boot_slot_count`
   plus a hash of the boot arena compared at adopt would make it mechanical. Attributed: the
   design ledger P1-2 decided SIGN covers it; the mechanism is still manual. [snapshot-coverage, high]

3. **Any live `await` blocks persistence (async rows still Pending)** —
   `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:8656–8686` (types at 4280, 4293, 4335;
   `sidetable.rs:558,568,803`). Claim: a machine holding an `AsyncAwait`/`AsyncGenerator*`/
   `FromAsync*` reaction or any live async generator is refused by the persist gate, so a
   worker suspended while awaiting a host response cannot be snapshotted. Evidence: `return
   Some("a promise reaction that would resume a non-persisted async frame");`; `assert_eq!
   (pending.len(), 3, ...)`. Why it matters: awaiting host I/O across cranks is the endo
   worker's central pattern; fail-closed, so not silent, but a functional gap. Known/open in
   the ledger; reported as still open. [snapshot-coverage, high]

4. **44 side tables × ~9 hand-parallel enumerations** —
   `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:43711–43755` (Hooks), `44001–44043` vs
   `44577–44644` (two prune lists), `44761–44941`. Claim: every new table needs coordinated
   edits in `gc_roots`, `extra_edges`, `swept`, the late-prune list, `ephemeron_edges`,
   `external_chunk_refs`, `each_side_table_ref_tail`, `free_pages`, the persist-gate holder
   walk, a snapshot verb pair and two registries; the registries only check *textual mention*,
   not subfield completeness. Evidence: `struct Hooks<'a> { functions: &'a mut …, // 39 borrows`.
   Why it matters: this is the mechanism behind every prior GC/snapshot miss; a per-table
   trait would turn nine lists into one impl per table. [modularity, medium]

5. **GC is boundary-only, and nothing asserts it** —
   `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:4082–4084` (also 3029–3031, registry:362,
   `collect_garbage` 43707). Claim: no code in `ironhorse-vm/src` calls `collect_garbage` or
   `free_pages`, so the root set's correctness for `pending_new_target` (DocumentedOnly) and
   every Rust-local `SlotIndex` temporary depends on collection never running mid-crank — a
   property the design does not state and the collector does not `debug_assert`. Evidence:
   `no mid-run GC runs, so the item value slots … are never swept underneath it (the stage-2
   GC roots contract)`. Why it matters: a future allocation-triggered collection (XS's actual
   behavior, and the only way to bound per-crank heap) would be unsound without a rooting
   discipline; the constraint should be stated in the design and asserted. [gc-roots, medium]

6. **Per-call and per-`try` heap allocation of scope state** —
   `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:4943–4959` (`CallerState`), `4971–4976`
   (`CatchJump`), `15667–15672`. Claim: every JS call allocates `Vec<Slot>`×2 + `HashMap` and
   every `try` entry clones the frame's `id_map` HashMap. Evidence: `id_map: self.id_map.clone(),`
   at the `CATCH` push. Why it matters: design § dispatch promises XS frame geometry and a
   small register struct; this is O(#named locals) allocation per `try` and three allocations
   per call, a direct threat to the 2.0x envelope. [performance, medium]

7. **Intl/Temporal is an oracle-blind surface in a consensus engine** —
   `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:3996–3999` (with 4420–4422, 3896–3915). Claim:
   nine Intl tables, four Temporal tables and a frozen `INTL_DATA_VERSION` ship although the
   design says Ironhorse omits Intl identically to XS, and by the code's own comment any
   program touching them "always aborts in the oracle", so neither results nor metering are
   ever differentially checked. Evidence: `such a program always aborts in the oracle at the
   missing Intl, so its metering is never compared`; `const INTL_DATA_VERSION: &str =
   "ironhorse-intl-2026a";`. Why it matters: the whole verification strategy is oracle
   result-parity; this surface has none, and its data version is a second consensus axis
   beside `ironhorse-meter-N`. [verification, medium]

8. **Both mechanical nets have a single-file type graph** —
   `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/tests/gc_visitation_registry.rs:34` (and 123–148, 446–457;
   `bulk.rs:119–122, 257–259`). Claim: a field whose type is defined outside `interp.rs`
   (`bulk::ArrayData`/`CollectionData` hold `Slot`s today) is opaque to the slot-bearing
   analysis, so `ValueSlotFree` passes vacuously for such value types and a future field like
   `foo: crate::bulk::X` with a non-slot key would be unclassified and silently pass.
   Evidence: `const SRC: &str = include_str!("../src/interp.rs");`. Why it matters: the nets
   are the compensating control for finding 4; their blind spot is exactly the direction
   (moving tables out of the monolith) the codebase should take. [verification, medium]

9. **Wide, convention-guarded restore/persist seam with panics inside checkpoint verbs** —
   `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:9232` (also 9120, 9181, 9205, 9526, 9666,
   3723–3725). Claim: several `restore_*` verbs accept arbitrary `u32` owners and return `()`,
   the arenas are `pub`, and snapshot verbs `expect`/index on cross-table pairing invariants,
   so a host composing the `pub` API out of order gets a panic mid-checkpoint rather than an
   error. Evidence: `.expect("every guest bytecode function owns a code segment")`;
   `let info = &self.functions[function];`. Why it matters: memory-safe, but the vm crate's
   public contract is "trust the caller", and a panic in a checkpoint verb loses the crank.
   [api-boundary, low]

10. **Five stale doc comments/attributes on the struct itself** —
    `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:4365–4369` (also 4340–4341, 4512–4516,
    4596–4604, 4647; `sidetable.rs:283–285`). Claim: the `regexps` doc denies the `last_index`
    field that `regexps_snapshot` serializes; `IntlTables` says bound functions are dropped
    while `IBFN` carries them; `then_id` and `bound_format` carry `#[allow(dead_code)]` while
    being read/written; `IteratorRow.iterable` is called weak while the collector edges it
    strongly. Evidence: `/// `lastIndex` is an ordinary own integer property of the instance,
    not stored here.` vs `last_index: f64,` (2820) and `r.last_index.to_bits(),` (9140).
    Why it matters: in a codebase whose completeness controls are doc-comment-driven
    registries, rotting field docs are a real hazard, not cosmetics. [docs-drift, low]

11. **Platform-width `usize` in exported snapshot rows, narrowed unchecked** —
    `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:4536` (and 4546; `image.rs:2915`). Claim: the
    Intl rows exported for the snapshot crate use `usize` where every other row uses fixed
    widths, and the encoder writes `(r.pos as u32)` without a bound check. Evidence:
    `pub segments: Vec<(usize, usize, bool)>,`; `pub pos: usize,`. Why it matters: unreachable
    in practice (bounded by string length) but inconsistent with the row-type discipline that
    makes the format host-independent. [api-boundary, low]

12. **`is_quiescent` asserts only the `.is_empty()` tables; the transient registers are argued, not checked** —
    `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:10733–10743` (with `sidetable.rs:716–731`).
    Claim: `env`, `result`, `pending_new_target`, `dispatch_depth`, `resume_status`,
    `callback_return_depth`, `active_segment` are classified provably-default at the boundary
    but no predicate or `debug_assert` at the persist gate checks them. Evidence: the predicate
    body checks `call_stack`, `stack`, `jumps`, `promise_jobs`, three run stacks, `exception`,
    `id_space_exhausted` only. Why it matters: a cheap mechanical strengthening of the
    contract the whole EmptyAtBoundary/transient argument rests on. [robustness, low]

13. **Script-level lexical bindings do not survive a crank** —
    `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:11234–11238` (with 11545–11546, field docs
    3806–3818). Claim: `locals`/`id_map` are cleared after every completed crank and only
    `var`s hoist to the global, so `let x = 1` in crank N is unresolvable in crank N+1;
    continuous and resumed machines agree, but whether XS's realm-global lexical environment
    persists across `xsRunScript` calls (the oracle's multi-crank behavior) is not established
    by anything in this region. Evidence: `self.locals.clear(); self.id_map.clear();` after
    `completed`. Why it matters: a multi-crank semantic the single-script parity bar cannot
    observe. Confidence: low on divergence, high on the ironhorse behavior. [other, low]

## What is done well

- Two independent, source-parsing completeness nets (`gc_visitation_registry.rs`,
  `sidetable.rs` tests) reconcile the struct's field list two-way against classifications and
  check the GC classification against the real visitor bodies — the right shape of control
  for a 165-field struct, and they visibly caught the wave-6 class.
- Every snapshot emitter sorts by owner/key and the GC root set is sorted+deduped, so
  `HashMap` iteration order never reaches bytes or trace order; the promise cluster is emitted
  in compacted, canonical form (4907–4920).
- The persist gate reasons from the *doomed set* ("which function slots does resume fail to
  bring back?") rather than enumerating holders (8688–8699) — the sound direction — and every
  `restore_*` verb documents exactly which structural violations it refuses.
- Weak-keyed tables have mechanically checked slot-free values and are pruned in both sweeps;
  chunk offsets held outside the arena (`static_str`, buffers, side-table string Slots) are
  enumerated for compaction with a clear comment on why each must be.
- Intl is fully host-independent by construction (frozen in-tree profile, `BTreeMap` for
  extension keys, closed-set decoders for `&'static str` keys and error names).
- Field-level doc comments are unusually explicit about *why* a table exists and which XS
  slot it replaces, which made this audit possible at all.
