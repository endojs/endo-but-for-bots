## Purpose and responsibilities

This region is the engine's *governance surface*: the documents that say what IronHorse is supposed to be (`designs/ironhorse-engine.md`, three sibling designs), the document that says what has landed (`rust/engine/README.md`), one in-tree handoff note, and the CI that decides which of those claims are mechanically enforced.

Responsibilities, as the artifacts themselves define them:

- `designs/ironhorse-engine.md` — the approved architecture (Status "Approved", design:7), the metering doctrine (design:361-467), the crate roster and `unsafe` budget (design:264-293, 581-598), memory-safety CI enforcement (design:600-618), test262/fuzz/endor-integration requirements (design:620-748), the staged roadmap and acceptance bars (design:772-923), Design Decisions (design:937-986), and ten binding Resolved Questions (design:988-1185).
- `rust/engine/README.md` — nominally the engine workspace README; in practice a per-child acceptance ledger (see § Architectural observations, "Information architecture").
- `designs/ironhorse-meter-opcode-cost-instrumentation.md` — "Not Started" (meter-design:7); the *only* named source of the meter's weights (design:400-411).
- `designs/ironhorse-test262-convergence.md` — corpus→test262 cases and the `endot-ih` runner; Status "In progress" (convergence:7).
- `designs/ironhorse-debugger-recovery-and-uncaught.md` — Proposed (debugger:8); stage-7 recovery plan.
- `rust/engine/ASYNC-AWAIT-HANDOFF.md` — a child-to-child implementation handoff, Status LANDED (handoff:3).
- `.github/workflows/ci.yml` `test-ironhorse` (ci:695-750) and `test-ironhorse-oracle` (ci:763-833), `build-xsnap` (ci:617-675, the only root-workspace Ironhorse test), `ironhorse-full-test262.yml` (dispatch-only sweep), `ironhorse-deep-fuzz.yml` (nightly, 4 targets).

## Architectural observations

**Information architecture of the README.** 2,953 lines / ~208 KB with 15 headings; two `##` sections hold 86% of the file: "Running the harness" is 951 lines (README:160-1110) and "Stage 5: the compiler port" is 1,584 lines (README:1304-2887). The "Running the harness" section is not about running the harness after line ~180; it is the stage-3/3b/4/4a/4b acceptance narrative, one child per paragraph, each paragraph often >40 lines with no sub-heading (e.g. README:181-480 is a single 300-line paragraph run). Stage 5 contains six successive "verify" blockquotes with mutually contradicting verdicts retained as dated history (README:1709 "NOT MET", 1969 "MET", 2167 "NOT MET", 2451 "NOT MET", 2660 "NOT MET", 2951 "MET"); the reader must know the ordering convention (newest first at 1656, then oldest-to-newest below) to find the current truth. The front-page crate table (README:15-23) is stale: `ironhorse-vm` is "interpreter over the stage-1 opcode subset" (README:17) though the file later records full 245-opcode coverage; `ironhorse-262` "runs the same bytecode" (README:19) though `Compiler::Ironhorse` is the default (README:22, 2896-2898). Effect: the document that a maintainer would open first cannot be trusted for current state, and there is no "current status" surface anywhere in the workspace — the design's Status is a single word "Approved" (design:7) with no `## Status` section as `designs/AGENTS.md` prescribes.

**Doctrine drift, stated precisely.** The 2026-07-04 revision made XS-computron parity a non-goal and made the weights "calibration constants derived from the cost-calibration instrumentation" (design:400-411). Everything landed after that date still targets bit-exact XS computrons (README 37×), the meter module says so in its own doc comment (meter.rs:3-4) while its next paragraph says the opposite (meter.rs:19-20), and the only mechanism that could produce non-XS weights is a design with Status "Not Started" (meter-design:7). The consequence is not a determinism bug — a frozen XS-derived table is as deterministic as any — but (a) the "accuracy" half of "accuracy over parity" has no evidence and no instrument, (b) the doc contract is self-contradictory, so a future maintainer cannot tell whether an XS-mismatched constant is a bug or a calibration, and (c) `ironhorse-meter-1` (introduced 2026-08-28 in `d8a2191e`, the same commit that touched 206 metering-constant lines in `interp.rs`; git history is squashed so no earlier bump history exists) names a table nobody calibrated. The `harden` paragraph (README:928-933) is the one place the README concedes "computron parity over a transitive harden is structurally unavailable" and falls back to a result-gated bar — evidence that parity, not accuracy, is the operative bar in practice.

**Two meters, one word.** The VM meter and the parse meter carry different version strings (`ironhorse-meter-1` vs `ironhorse-meter-0`) and the parse meter's output is consumed by nothing (compile `lib.rs:52` computes it; `ironhorse_engine.rs:162` never reads it). A "release" in the design's sense (design:380-386) is therefore not a single identifiable thing in code.

**CI shape vs. claims.** PR CI is `cargo test` on two pure-Rust crates plus one root test; the oracle job is `cargo test` on four crates. There is no `cargo clippy`, no `cargo fmt --check`, no Miri, no sanitizer, no cross-platform matrix, no determinism repeat gate, no differential fuzz lane — for a codebase whose governing design names Miri+ASAN/UBSAN as "CI enforcement" (design:612-615) and whose consensus story is "identical computrons on every host, architecture, and build" (design:373-375). Observed locally: `cargo fmt --all -- --check` reports 1,315 diff hunks across every crate (469 vm, 384 snapshot, 297 compile, 99 262, 25 fuzz, 25 regexp, 16 xs-oracle); the census records 125 clippy warnings and one deny-level clippy error (`intl_number.rs:779`). Neither is a correctness bug; both mean the repo has no mechanical style/lint floor, which for a 45k-line single-file `impl` is a maintainability multiplier.

**Path-filter blind spot.** Both engine jobs self-skip on PRs unless the diff matches `^rust/engine/|^rust/endo/ironhorse-store-sqlite/|^Cargo\.toml$|^Cargo\.lock$|^\.github/workflows/ci\.yml$` (ci:724; the oracle job adds `c/moddable`, `.gitmodules`, `xsnap-platform.*` at ci:793). `packages/test262-runner/test262/test/ironhorse/**` — 1,712 cases that `ironhorse-262/tests/regressions_dual_run.rs` and the retired-corpus equivalence tests gate (README:1288-1290; `compile_diff.rs:414`) — is not in either regex, so a PR that edits or deletes a regression case runs neither job. The `ironhorse-store-sqlite` path *is* included, showing the probe was extended when a new input was recognized; the corpus move was not recognized.

**Oracle-uncheckable surface admitted by design.** Intl (five constructors) is the first large surface whose correctness cannot be differentially checked (`intl_number.rs:3-6`), depends on external Unicode/CLDR data tables (`icu_*` crates, pinned `=2.2.0`), and per the design's own rule is "meter-affecting and therefore version-gated behind a meter bump" (design:1183-1185). No amendment to resolved question 10 exists, so the governing document says the opposite of the code. The determinism lens should treat any `icu_*` version bump as consensus-affecting; nothing in the docs says so.

**Fuzz process is out-of-repo.** README:134-137 delegates continuous fuzzing to "a garden background service … (design: `designs/continuous-ironhorse-fuzz.md` in kriscendobot/garden)" — a document and process in another repository, unverifiable from this one. In-repo, the nightly lane runs 4 of 9 targets and its header (deep-fuzz.yml:1-8) describes a PR smoke lane that no longer exists.

**Handoff docs as process artifacts.** `ASYNC-AWAIT-HANDOFF.md` sits at the workspace root, is marked LANDED (handoff:3), cites the superseded pin `48ee02d8cfe0` (handoff:34), embeds a personal path `TMPDIR=/home/kris/tmp` (handoff:140), and orchestration-budget prose ("2400s handler", handoff:27-32). Its GC-roots contract (handoff:163-169) duplicates design:910-923 in a different formulation; neither cites `gc_visitation_registry.rs`. The design family generally does not follow `designs/AGENTS.md`: `ironhorse-engine.md` uses `Revised` rows instead of `Updated` and `designs/README.md:355` shows Updated "—" despite amendments dated 2026-07-04/06/17/29; the convergence design's Status text was rewritten but carries no Updated field and still cites pin `48ee02d8cfe0` (convergence:26, 75) and the deleted `corpora/` (convergence:29-41); `designs/README.md:1283` names the runner `ironhorse-xst` while the design and code say `endot-ih`.

**Determinism/perf/error-model notes for other lenses (observed in passing, not audited here).** README:216-217 claims "the pin's exact libm choices" for `Math` — libm is platform-dependent unless a pure-Rust implementation is used; with a single-platform CI this is unverified. `interp.rs:3178` and `:4549-4570` reference time zones for `Intl.DateTimeFormat`/Temporal (host time/locale lens). No `[profile]` overflow-checks setting exists in either `Cargo.toml` (census) — release builds wrap silently, which the docs never mention.

## Candidate findings

Ordered most architecturally significant first. Confidence is stated per item.

1. **Metering doctrine is inverted in code and in the meter's own doc comment** — `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/meter.rs:3-4` vs `:19-20`.
   Claim: the shipped cost table is XS's weights (parity), not a calibrated Ironhorse table, and the only instrument that could produce one is unbuilt.
   Evidence: `//! A u64 in 16.16 fixed point, incremented at exactly XS's points with exactly XS's weights` (meter.rs:3-4); `/// Ironhorse's meter is its **own** frozen cost table, not a back-fit of the oracle's` (meter.rs:19-20); `| **Status** | Not Started` (meter-design:7).
   Why it matters: the design's "accuracy" property has zero evidence; `ironhorse-meter-1` labels an uncalibrated table; a maintainer cannot tell whether an XS-mismatched constant is a defect or a recalibration. Confidence: high.

2. **Miri/ASAN/UBSAN "CI enforcement" does not exist; Miri-named tests run as ordinary tests** — `designs/ironhorse-engine.md:612-615`; `ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:42664, 42819, 42857, 42900`.
   Claim: no workflow runs Miri or a sanitizer, so the memory-safety confidence claim rests on `forbid(unsafe_code)` alone.
   Evidence: `CI enforcement: Miri on the arena and GC test suites, ASAN/UBSAN on the oracle harness` (design:612-614); grep `miri|sanitizer|ASAN|UBSAN` over `.github/workflows` → no matches; `fn harden_transitive_freeze_is_miri_clean()` (interp.rs:42664) has no `cfg(miri)` and runs under `cargo test`.
   Why it matters: `forbid(unsafe_code)` does not cover the C oracle (the process that compiles hostile test262 input in CI) and README's five "Miri-clean" statements read as verified when they are aspirational. Confidence: high.

3. **`Intl` landed against a binding resolved question with no amendment and no oracle** — `ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:6409`; `designs/ironhorse-engine.md:1181-1185`.
   Claim: a five-constructor ECMA-402 surface exists in a consensus engine, is oracle-uncheckable by construction, depends on external `icu_*` data crates, and the governing design says the opposite.
   Evidence: `self.intrinsics.insert("Intl", intl);` (interp.rs:6409); `//! The pinned Moddable XS oracle has no ECMA-402 host, so every Intl case is proven by requiring ironhorse to run it … while the oracle reports the missing Intl binding` (intl_number.rs:3-6); `10. **Intl is omitted (intl402 stays out), matching the oracle.** … no seam is reserved for it` (design:1181-1185).

## What is done well

- **Fail-closed meter versioning at the persistence boundary.** `COST_TABLE_VERSION` is stamped into the `METR` atom and checked on resume/migration before any mutation (`store.rs:2010`, `image.rs:4444`, `tests/migration.rs:126-204`), with a test that panics if a refused store is touched. This is the one place the "release-versioned meter" is mechanically real, and it is done correctly.
- **Honest named skips over silent wrongness, consistently.** Across the README (e.g. README:200-205, 640-648, 865-879, 1002-1023) the discipline "a reference is a NAMED skip, never a completion divergence or a wrong value" is stated and, per the ledgers, enforced by `Halt::Unsupported` self-naming; the daemon seam carries it through (`ironhorse_engine.rs:14-17, 86-99`).
- **CI probes are defensively written.** The change probes fail open when the merge-commit shape is unexpected (ci:718-719) and avoid the `grep -q`/SIGPIPE false-miss (ci:720-725); the oracle fetch retries and fails loud (ci:804-809); the fuzz lockfile freshness check (deep-fuzz.yml:100-109) and digit-checked dispatch input (deep-fuzz.yml:123-127) show zizmor-aware authoring; `build-xsnap` pins `--features ironhorse-engine` explicitly to avoid a 0-test green (ci:671-675).
- **The whole-tree sweep is engineered for honesty and resumability.** Run-identity-stamped batches, plan-bound aggregation (never a glob), quarantine as an explicit `infrastructure` category, and a lede that discloses the SES/strict axes it does not measure (scripts README:9-14, 52-81).
- **The measurement-process corrections are recorded, not hidden.** The "binding process rule (s16 finding): a whole-tree claim requires the whole-tree enumeration" (README:1774-1783, 1981-1989) was adopted after an over-claim and is now visibly applied; earlier wrong verdicts are retained as dated history rather than rewritten.
- **The debugger design is unusually rigorous.** It re-verifies every research claim against the current head, rejects a tempting fix (`mxArgumentsFlag` propagation) with a byte-level reason, and names its prerequisite as gating rather than a follow-up (debugger:23-26, 195-223, 349-365).
