# Lens: snapshot and persistence seam

HEAD 97d8de25. Every claim below was verified by reading the cited lines in the current
tree. Three claims were additionally **executed** in a scratch crate outside the repo
(`scratchpad/probe2`, path-deps on the engine crates, reusing the engine target dir;
`git status` clean afterwards); their output is quoted verbatim.

## 1. Assessment

The seam is, structurally, one of the strongest parts of this engine. There is a single
validated-image type (`ValidatedSnapshot`, image.rs:411-437) that restore cannot be
reached without; one semantic bounds gate (`check_image_slot_bounds`, image.rs:3478)
shared by the container path, the eager store path and the lazy attach path; a
ledger (`sidetable.rs`) whose classification is reconciled *mechanically* against
`Interp`'s field list parsed from source; and a determinism discipline (every emitter
owner-sorted, numbers as raw bits, `BTreeMap`/`BTreeSet` throughout) that I could not
break by inspection. The persist gate has the right shape — it computes the *doomed set*
("which function slots does resume fail to bring back?") and traverses what is written,
rather than enumerating holders — and its O(dirty) checkpoint form carries a written
induction argument that holds up when you follow it (interp.rs:8631-8639; the doomed set
is provably empty immediately after a resume, so the dirty-only walk cannot miss a
reference minted before the resume).

The seam's failures are not in what it validates but in **where its preconditions live**
and in **which states its instrument can observe**.

The load-bearing precondition is `is_quiescent` (interp.rs:10733). Its doc says a halted
crank "returns with pending microtasks, a populated call stack, live handlers, a set
exception, and a mid-frame value stack", and the predicate tests exactly those. That
description is false for a whole class of halts: a `MeterAbort` or `StepLimit` at a
**top-level** loop-closing check returns straight out of `dispatch_at_inner` with all nine
tested fields at their empty/default values, while `locals`, `id_map` and `result` — GC
roots that `run` clears only `if completed` (interp.rs:11232-11238) — stay live. I ran it:
`write_snapshot` succeeds on such a machine, and after one boundary collection the
continuous machine and its resumed twin hold **different live counts, different free lists
and different canonical snapshot bytes, permanently**. That is the exact divergence class
(W6-11) the completed-crank hygiene was added to close, still open on the halted path, and
it is a durable-identity fork, not merely a lost register.

The second structural gap is that the gate is attached to three *convenience verbs*
(`write_snapshot`, `begin_store_session`, `checkpoint_to_store`) rather than to the data
path. `snapshot_image`, `write_machine`, `image_to_batch` and `HeapStore::commit` are all
`pub` and ungated, and `MachineSnapshot::persist_gate` defaults to `Ok(())` for any other
implementor. Nothing downstream can recover the property: a Pending row (a live `await`,
an async generator) is simply *absent* from the image, so no reader can tell a gated image
from an ungated one. Fail-closed here is a convention over a public API, not an invariant.

Third, canonicality — the stated basis for the SHA-256 CAS key being an identity — is
enforced at the atom envelope and in the 24 side-table decoders, but **not inside the six
oldest atom payloads**. I appended one byte inside the `HEAP` payload of an honest
container: it was accepted, hashed differently, and re-emitted to the honest bytes. The
store-side sibling is worse, because `small` bytes feed the Merkle root.

Fourth, the free-record exemption in the bounds gate is one predicate short. Free records
are (correctly) treated as opaque, but a **live** edge *into* a free record is not
refused, and the collector then walks the free record's stale, unvalidated payload. I
crafted such a container: `read_validated_machine` accepted it and `collect_garbage`
panicked out of bounds — an anonymous release panic, exactly what the branch's own
`a_lazily_resumed_poisoned_store_dies_named_at_the_fault` test exists to prevent.

Finally, the verification instrument has blind spots that line up with the defects. The
shared backend suite never reopens a store (so durability is untestable through it) and
never issues a refused commit (so the trait's split-brain and geometry obligations are
untested through it); the metamorphic suite asserts every crank *completes*, so it can
never reach the halted-crank state; and the production embedding
(`rust/endo/src/ironhorse_engine.rs`) never arms the meter at all, so "computrons
invariant across suspend points" is locked only by hand-written tests that call
`rearm_meter`/`reattach_meter_host` themselves.

Version gating is in good shape: `VERS` range, `SIGN` equality, `METR` cost-table
(`ironhorse-meter-1`), store schema 23 with a min-supported 5 and a verify-then-restamp
ladder, and a strict-advance guard. The soft spots are `SIGN`'s boot-layout half (a human
convention, `boot_slot_count` unserialized) and the manifest fields outside the root.

## 2. Findings

### F1. `is_quiescent` admits a meter/step-aborted top-level crank; persisting it forks the machine's durable identity — CRITICAL

- **File:line:** `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:10733` (predicate), `:11232-11238`
  (`run`'s conditional clear), consumed at `ironhorse-snapshot/src/machine.rs:199-207`,
  `:963-970`, `:1064-1067`.
- **Category:** determinism · **Confidence:** high (reproduced) · **Novelty:** new
  (the store-seam ledger's invariant #1, "every persist verb gates on `is_quiescent`", is
  upheld for the *verbs*; the predicate itself is what is wrong. Independently surfaced by
  the `snapshot-container-machine-ledger` mapper this wave.)
- **Claim:** a crank that halts with `MeterAbort`/`StepLimit` at a top-level loop-closing
  check passes `is_quiescent()` and every persist verb, and the resulting snapshot's
  resumed twin permanently diverges from the uninterrupted machine in free list, live
  count and canonical snapshot bytes.
- **Evidence** (predicate and the clear it depends on):
  ```rust
  pub fn is_quiescent(&self) -> bool {
      self.call_stack.is_empty() && self.stack.is_empty() && self.jumps.is_empty()
          && self.promise_jobs.is_empty() && self.gen_run_stack.is_empty()
          && self.async_run_stack.is_empty() && self.async_gen_run_stack.is_empty()
          && self.exception.kind == Kind::Undefined && !self.id_space_exhausted
  }
  ```
  ```rust
  // A HALTED crank keeps everything: the quiescence gate refuses it ...
  if completed { self.result = Slot::undefined(); self.locals.clear(); self.id_map.clear(); }
  ```
  Probe output (`let i = 0; let big = [...]; while (true) { i = i + 1; }` under
  `arm_meter(1, |_| false)`):
  ```
  A: completed=false halt=MeterAbort
  A: is_quiescent=true unpersistable=None
  A: write_snapshot OK (23143 bytes)
  A3: after a boundary collection: continuous live=715 free=2 | resumed live=714 free=3
  A3: post-collection snapshot bytes identical = false (continuous 23349, resumed 23137)
  A3: after the next crank, snapshot bytes identical = false
  ```
- **Impact:** a supervisor that snapshots after a metered abort (the natural response to a
  computron-budget abort) commits a torn epoch that validates cleanly on every path. The
  two replicas answer the same results and computrons on the next crank while their
  durable CAS keys have forked — the "identical per-crank results and computrons hiding
  it" failure the `cranks` field's own doc names as the thing to avoid. `locals`/`id_map`
  are also GC roots, so the divergence is in allocation order, which the design treats as
  replica-visible.
- **Recommendation:** add an explicit `last_crank_completed` latch set by `run` and test it
  in `is_quiescent` (cheapest and exactly right: quiescence is a *lifecycle* property, not
  a table-emptiness property). Failing that, add `locals`/`id_map`/`result` to the
  predicate *and* to `sidetable.rs`'s `empty_at_boundary` reconciliation, so the ledger's
  "transients are empty at the boundary" audit trail becomes checked rather than argued.
  Extend `persist_gates.rs` (today only the `throw` shape, :35-44) with a meter-abort and a
  step-limit shape, and let one metamorphic scenario contain a halted crank.

### F2. A live heap edge into a free record bypasses the bounds gate; the collector then walks the free record's unvalidated payload — HIGH

- **File:line:** `rust/engine/ironhorse-snapshot/src/image.rs:3528` (the `check` closure),
  `:3546-3548` (free records skipped). Other sites:
  `ironhorse-vm/src/value.rs:618-627` (same exemption on the lazy fault path),
  `ironhorse-vm/src/gc.rs:137-140`, `ironhorse-vm/src/value.rs:906-909`.
- **Category:** robustness · **Confidence:** high (reproduced) · **Novelty:** new (not in
  the store-seam ledger; the free-record *opacity* reasoning at image.rs:3499-3509 is what
  opens it).
- **Claim:** a container whose live slot references a free record, and whose free record
  carries a stale out-of-arena `Reference`, passes `read_validated_machine` and panics with
  an anonymous index-out-of-bounds at the first `collect_garbage`.
- **Evidence:**
  ```rust
  let check = |s: &Slot| -> Result<(), SnapshotError> {
      let mut bad = false;
      s.each_ref_slot(|r| bad |= !r.is_null() && r.0 >= slot_count);
  ```
  ```rust
  for (i, s) in heap.iter().enumerate() {
      if is_free(i as u32) { continue; // opaque: dead bytes, preserved for index identity only }
      check(s)?;
  }
  ```
  `SlotArena::mark` indexes `self.marks[i]` directly (value.rs:906). Probe output:
  ```
  C: crafted live->free->out-of-arena container ACCEPTED by read_validated_machine
  C: collect_garbage panicked = true
  ```
- **Impact:** this is a regression of the branch's own stated bar. `machine.rs:1700-1723`
  (`a_lazily_resumed_poisoned_store_dies_named_at_the_fault`) and `:1730-1760` require a
  poisoned reference/chunk offset to die *named*, and assert that "the CONTAINER path
  refuses this exact content". Hiding the poison one hop away, behind a free record,
  defeats both. The same crafted shape also feeds a bogus offset to `ChunkArena::compact`
  through `live_offsets` (gc.rs:172-181), whose asserts are the other named-panic class.
- **Recommendation:** one predicate. In `check`, refuse a live edge into a free record:
  `bad |= !r.is_null() && (r.0 >= slot_count || is_free(r.0))`, applied to heap records,
  the stack and every side-table value. This is honest-safe by construction — the sweep
  frees only unmarked records and every edge of a marked record is marked, so no honest
  post-GC image contains a live→free edge — and it additionally closes the aliasing case
  where a live reference names a slot `alloc` will hand out again. Mirror it in the lazy
  fault gate (value.rs:618-652), which has the same exemption. Lock it with a crafted-row
  refusal beside the existing free-record hygiene tests (image.rs:5329-5361).

### F3. The persist gate is attached to three convenience verbs, not to the data path; every constructor it protects is `pub` and ungated — HIGH

- **File:line:** `rust/engine/ironhorse-snapshot/src/machine.rs:209` (`snapshot_image`, no
  gate), `:139-141` (`persist_gate` default `Ok(())`), `store.rs:2733`
  (`pub fn image_to_batch`), `image.rs:4210` (`pub fn write_machine`), `store.rs:1777`
  (`fn commit` on the trait).
- **Category:** api-boundary · **Confidence:** high · **Novelty:** new (the ledger's
  invariant #1 is stated and verified of the *verbs*).
- **Claim:** `store::image_to_batch(&interp.snapshot_image(&sig), 1, "")` followed by
  `store.commit(&batch)` persists a machine that neither quiescence nor the Pending-row
  gate ever saw, and no downstream reader can detect it.
- **Evidence:**
  ```rust
  fn persist_gate(&self) -> Result<(), MachineSnapshotError> { Ok(()) }
  ```
  ```rust
  fn snapshot_image(&self, signature: &Signature) -> MachineImage {   // no gate
  ```
  ```rust
  pub fn image_to_batch(image: &MachineImage, epoch: u64, prev_seal: &str) -> CheckpointBatch
  ```
  `validate_store` (store.rs:3167-3172) can only catch the sub-case where the value stack
  is non-empty; a Pending row is *absent* from the image, so absence is indistinguishable
  from "the machine had none".
- **Impact:** the entire silent-wrong class the W6-9 Pending gate exists to prevent (a
  resumed proxy that degrades to a plain object, an async generator that loses its row) is
  reachable through the crate's own public API, and `MachineSnapshot` is a public trait
  whose default implementation opts out. The engine's own test helpers already use
  `snapshot_image` + `image_to_batch` directly (machine.rs:1700-1760), so the pattern is
  established.
- **Recommendation:** make the gate structural. `snapshot_image` should return a
  `GatedImage` (or `Result<MachineImage, _>`) that only a gated call can produce, with
  `image_to_batch`/`write_machine` taking it — the same proof-token pattern
  `ValidatedSnapshot` already applies on the read side. Remove the permissive
  `persist_gate` default, or make the trait sealed.

### F4. Core atom payloads accept trailing slack, so one machine has many valid containers and the SHA-256 CAS key is not an identity — HIGH

- **File:line:** `rust/engine/ironhorse-snapshot/src/image.rs:807` (`decode_heap`),
  `:731-732` (`decode_strings`, no end check), `:4199` (`decode_stack`), `:131`
  (`CreationParams::decode`), `format.rs:251` (`Version::decode`). Store-side sibling:
  `store.rs:1439-1447` + the same inner decoders at `:1452-1455`.
- **Category:** verification · **Confidence:** high (reproduced) · **Novelty:**
  partially-known — the ledger's **L6 / P3-2** was assessed "FIXED, ledger stale" by the
  prior mapper; the fix covered the *envelope and atom grammar* only, and the class it
  claims to close is still open one layer down.
- **Claim:** appending a byte inside the `HEAP` (or `STAC`, `KEYS`, `NAME`, `CREA`, `VERS`)
  payload yields a container `read_machine` accepts, whose SHA-256 differs, and which
  re-emits to the honest bytes.
- **Evidence:**
  ```rust
  if p.len() - i < want { return Err(SnapshotError::Corrupt("HEAP records truncated")); }
  ```
  ```rust
  if p.len() - 4 < want { return Err(SnapshotError::Corrupt("STAC records truncated")); }
  ```
  ```rust
  if p.len() < 8 { return Err(SnapshotError::Corrupt("CREA payload too short")); }
  ```
  Probe output:
  ```
  B: HEAP+1 ACCEPTED; bytes differ from honest = true; re-emit == honest = true
  ```
  `crafted_row_refusals.rs:279-291` states the rule ("one logical machine must have exactly
  ONE container encoding, or its SHA-256 is not an identity") and tests five shapes, all at
  the envelope/atom level.
- **Impact:** the CAS key is the supervisor's identity for a suspended worker and an
  ephemeral GC root; a second key for one machine breaks dedup and any equality-of-state
  reasoning built on it. On the store side the same slack is inside `small` bytes, which
  are hashed into the Merkle root — so one logical small state has many roots, alongside
  the *separate* empty-section double encoding (`store.rs:1455-1465` accepts a zero-length
  section as the empty table while `image.rs:4346-4351` refuses a present-but-empty atom).
- **Recommendation:** give the six oldest decoders the same `Cursor::done()` exact-consumption
  rule the 24 side-table decoders already have (`decode_heap`: `p.len() - i == want`;
  `decode_strings`/`decode_u32s`: `i == p.len()`; `CreationParams`/`Version`: `==`, not `<`).
  Then extend the fuzz invariance arm (`ironhorse-fuzz/src/snapshot.rs:1246-1258`) to assert
  `write(read(b)) == b` on **accepted mutated** inputs, not only on writer-produced bytes —
  today that arm checks only "no panic", which is why this survived.

### F5. Metering does not survive a resume in the shipped path; `arm_meter` zeroes the restored computron index — HIGH

- **File:line:** `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:10668` (`arm_meter`, resets index),
  `:10680-10683` (the doc's own warning), `:10699` (`reattach_meter_host`),
  `:11201-11205` (`check_meter` returns `Continue` when `meter_host` is `None`);
  absence at `rust/endo/src/ironhorse_engine.rs` (no `arm_meter`/`rearm_meter`/
  `reattach_meter_host` anywhere in `rust/endo`).
- **Category:** metering · **Confidence:** high · **Novelty:** new (W6-13 added the two
  preserving forms and locked them in `persist_gates.rs:139-246`; the embedding gap and the
  `arm_meter` foot-gun are not in the ledger).
- **Claim:** a resumed `Interp` has `meter_host == None` while its restored `interval` is
  non-zero, so it reports armed state and never aborts; and the one arming form a cold
  caller naturally reaches (`arm_meter`) resets the restored computron index to 0.
- **Evidence:**
  ```rust
  /// The host callback cannot travel in a snapshot, so every resume that wants metering
  /// MUST call one of the three arm forms — a restored machine that skips them reports
  /// armed state but never consults a host.
  ```
  ```rust
  fn check_meter(&mut self) -> MeterCheck {
      match self.meter_host.as_mut() { Some(host) => self.meter.check(host), None => MeterCheck::Continue }
  }
  ```
  `grep -rn "arm_meter|rearm_meter|reattach_meter_host" rust/` returns only `ironhorse-262`,
  `machine.rs:1837` and `persist_gates.rs` — no hit in `rust/endo`.
- **Impact:** the lens claim "computrons invariant across suspend points" is not exercised
  by any shipped caller: `PersistentMachine` runs unmetered, so `Halt::MeterAbort` is dead
  in production and a hostile guest is bounded only by `step_limit` (`u64::MAX` in
  production per interp.rs:11318-11325). A future embedding that arms with `arm_meter` after
  a resume silently restarts the budget — a consensus divergence between a suspended and an
  uninterrupted replica that no test would catch.
- **Recommendation:** have `image_to_interp` leave the machine in an explicitly
  *disarmed-but-was-armed* state that `run` refuses (or that a `meter_armed()` query
  reports honestly), and make `arm_meter` reject a machine whose meter state was restored,
  forcing the caller to choose `rearm_meter` or `reattach_meter_host` deliberately. Add a
  metamorphic scenario that arms the meter, so the seven-way computron equality actually
  covers the metered path.

### F6. The shared backend acceptance suite cannot observe durability or any refused commit — MEDIUM

- **File:line:** `rust/engine/ironhorse-snapshot/src/store_suite.rs:346`
  (`metamorphic_suite`), `:505` (`lazy_working_set_bound`), `:541`
  (`checkpoint_acceptance`), `:600` (`resume_equals_uninterrupted`); contract at
  `store.rs:1774-1777`.
- **Category:** verification · **Confidence:** high · **Novelty:** new (ledger **L13**
  covers only the evict-schedule half).
- **Claim:** no entry point in the suite drops a store handle and re-acquires the same
  backend from its medium, and every commit it issues is `.expect("checkpoint")` on a
  legitimate successor — so a backend that buffered commits in RAM, or that skipped
  `check_succession`/`check_batch` entirely, passes the one instrument the design says
  every backend must run.
- **Evidence:** `grep -n "reopen|FileStore::open|drop(store" store_suite.rs` returns
  nothing; the four `pub fn` signatures take `impl FnMut() -> S` (an *empty*-store factory)
  or `&mut dyn HeapStore`. The trait's obligation is prose:
  ```rust
  /// Apply one checkpoint atomically. Must enforce the epoch
  /// discipline via [`check_epoch`] and drop rows beyond the new geometry.
  fn commit(&mut self, batch: &CheckpointBatch) -> Result<(), StoreError>;
  ```
- **Impact:** durability and split-brain refusal are exactly what a new backend gets wrong,
  and their coverage today is hand-copied per backend (`store_file.rs:887-903`,
  `store_checkpoint.rs:158-274`, the SQLite crate's own tests) — the duplication the shared
  suite exists to eliminate. The SQLite backend is the production one and lives outside this
  workspace.
- **Recommendation:** add an optional `reopen: &mut dyn FnMut(S) -> S` to the suite and a
  `commit_contract(store: &mut dyn HeapStore)` entry point that replays an epoch, forks a
  seal, commits into a non-empty store and omits a grown row (all constructible today with
  `image_to_batch` + `reseal_batch`). Better still, give `HeapStore` a provided `commit`
  that runs `check_succession` + `apply_batch` and delegates to a narrow
  `persist_verified(...)` hook, so the gauntlet stops being a per-backend convention.

### F7. The metamorphic suite varies the suspend *backend* but barely varies the suspend *point*, and asserts every crank completes — MEDIUM

- **File:line:** `rust/engine/ironhorse-snapshot/src/store_suite.rs:60-66` (baseline
  `assert!(o.completed, ...)`), `:97-112` (blob variant suspends between *every* crank),
  `:133-215` (store variants, likewise), `:224-260` (variant 7: checkpoint every crank,
  resume once at the end).
- **Category:** verification · **Confidence:** high · **Novelty:** new.
- **Claim:** the seven ways differ in backend and residency schedule, but the suspend
  schedule takes only three values — never, every boundary, and once at the end — and no
  scenario contains a halted crank, so the state F1 exploits is unreachable by the suite.
- **Evidence:**
  ```rust
  assert!(o.completed, "{scenario} baseline crank {} completes (halt: {:?})", i + 1, o.halt);
  ```
  ```rust
  for (i, (bytecode, _)) in compiled.iter().enumerate() {
      if i > 0 { let bytes = m.write_snapshot(&sig())...; m = from_snapshot_bytes(&bytes, &sig())...; }
  ```
- **Impact:** the design's transparency claim is "results and computrons invariant across
  suspend points"; the instrument proves it across *backends and residency schedules* with
  the suspend point held nearly constant. Two of the three most consequential axes — an
  arbitrary subset of boundaries, and a boundary reached by a halt rather than a completion
  — are unsampled.
- **Recommendation:** parameterize the suspend schedule (for `k` cranks, run all `2^k`
  suspend subsets for small `k`, or a seeded pseudo-random subset for larger ones — the
  suite is already generic over the backend factory), and add a scenario whose middle crank
  halts under a refusing meter with the expectation that persist is *refused* (which will
  fail today, and should).

### F8. `cranks` and `epoch` sit outside the Merkle root, and the seal is never verified at open — MEDIUM

- **File:line:** `rust/engine/ironhorse-snapshot/src/store.rs:201-221` (the `cranks` doc),

- **Category:** determinism · **Confidence:** high (code); impact requires at-rest

- **Claim:** a length-preserving byte flip of `cranks` in a stored manifest passes
  `validate_store` and changes when the resumed replica next runs `partial_collect`,
  producing exactly the fork the field's own doc says it exists to prevent.
- **Evidence:**
  ```rust

  /// makes it resume-invariant ... a session-local `cranks_since_collect` that `open()`
  /// zeroed made an ordinary suspend/resume fork the durable heap, with identical
  /// per-crank results and computrons hiding it.
  ```
  ```rust

  /// and the row leaves, and the manifest is in neither
  ```
  while `root`'s own doc claims "a length-preserving byte flip at rest fails closed"
  (store.rs:221-229).
- **Impact:** the same silent fork the wave-5 measurement found, reachable through the one

### F9. `SIGN`'s boot-layout half is a human convention; `boot_slot_count` is never serialized — MEDIUM

- **File:line:** `rust/engine/ironhorse-snapshot/src/format.rs:327-345`,
  `machine.rs:635-636` (`Interp::new()` then arena swap),
  `ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:3738` (`boot_slot_count`, in no `*_snapshot`).
- **Category:** snapshot-coverage · **Confidence:** high · **Novelty:** known-open —
  ledger invariant #5 / P1-2, recorded as "Answered and implemented"; it is implemented as
  a *process rule*, not a mechanism.
- **Claim:** two builds whose `create_intrinsics` allocate different slot layouts, handed
  the same host signature string, adopt each other's containers and bind this build's
  boot-derived maps (`functions`, every `*_proto`, `intrinsics`) to the image's unrelated
  slots, silently.
- **Evidence:**
  ```rust
  ///    written under a different boot layout would therefore attach this
  ///    build's boot metadata to the image's unrelated slots — silently.
  ///    Nothing else catches it: `boot_slot_count` is not serialized, and
  ///    `VERS` versions the WIRE SCHEMA (the atom set), not the heap the atoms describe.
  ```
  ```rust
  pub fn is_compatible_with(&self, current: &Signature) -> bool { self == current }
  ```
  The lock (`crafted_row_refusals.rs:246-277`) only proves two *different strings*
  mismatch.
- **Impact:** the design's cross-build promise ("survive daemon replacement and compatible
  engine upgrades") rests on a maintainer remembering to edit a string. This branch has
  re-pinned the golden vector for boot-heap changes at least four times (see the comment
  block at `metamorphic_determinism.rs:95-120`), which is the empirical rate at which that
  memory would be tested.
- **Recommendation:** serialize `boot_slot_count` and a digest of the fresh boot arena in
  `CREA` (or a new atom) and compare at read. It is cheap, mechanical, and turns a process
  rule into a gate.

### F10. Three Pending rows make every `await`-bearing or async-generator-bearing machine un-checkpointable — MEDIUM

- **File:line:** `rust/engine/ironhorse-snapshot/src/sidetable.rs:558`, `:568`, `:586`;
  gates at `ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:8656-8668` and `:8680-8686`.
- **Category:** snapshot-coverage · **Confidence:** high · **Novelty:** known-open (ledger
  **L8**; still exactly three Pending rows).
- **Claim:** `checkpoint_to_store`/`write_snapshot` refuse by name whenever any live
  promise carries a reaction whose kind is not `User`/`FinallyReturn`/`Combine`, or any
  non-free `async_generators` owner exists.
- **Evidence:**
  ```rust
  if async_reaction { return Some("a promise reaction that would resume a non-persisted async frame"); }
  ```
  ```rust
  if self.async_generators.keys().any(|owner| !self.slots.is_free_index(*owner)) {
      return Some("an async generator whose state does not yet persist");
  }
  ```
- **Impact:** fail-closed and correct, but it excludes the daemon's central pattern — a vat
  suspended while awaiting a host response. Combined with F5 (the worker envelope is
  dependency-gated, ledger **L9**), the persistence stack has no production caller that
  exercises it end to end. The gate is also *conservative* in the useful direction: it scans
  `self.promises.values()` including unreachable promises, so it cannot be evaded by
  withholding a collection.
- **Recommendation:** no change to the gate; the architectural note is that the ledger's
  three rows are the product boundary, and the `async_instances` carry should be sequenced
  before the worker envelope rather than after it, or the envelope will land on a seam that
  refuses its own workload.

### F11. Two container grammars over one row set, with divergent emptiness rules and two hand-maintained wiring lists — MEDIUM

- **File:line:** `rust/engine/ironhorse-snapshot/src/image.rs:4346-4351`
  (`present_and_non_empty`) vs `store.rs:1455-1465` (zero-length section ⇒ empty table);
  `image.rs:3477` (`check_image_slot_bounds`, 16 positional params),
  `machine.rs:432-456` (`restore_side_tables`, 22 params), `store.rs:1387`
  (`[Vec<u8>; 30]`).
- **Category:** modularity · **Confidence:** high · **Novelty:** new (image-a/image-b
  mappers surfaced the same shape; not in the ledger).
- **Claim:** every side table is wired twice, under two different canonicality rules, across
  at least nine hand-maintained sites, and no test derives coverage from `MachineImage`'s
  field list.
- **Evidence:**
  ```rust
  fn present_and_non_empty<T>(rows: &[T], what: &'static str) -> Result<(), SnapshotError> {
      if rows.is_empty() { return Err(SnapshotError::Corrupt(what)); }
  ```
  ```rust
  let arrays = if arrays_bytes.is_empty() { Vec::new() } else { crate::image::decode_arrays(arrays_bytes)? };
  ```
  The VM has a struct-derived net for GC roots (`tests/gc_visitation_registry.rs`) and the
  ledger has one for `Interp` fields (`sidetable.rs:662-798`); there is none tying
  `MachineImage`'s 36 fields to `CANONICAL_ATOM_ORDER`, `LangRows`, the gate, the store
  sections and `restore_side_tables`.
- **Impact:** this is the mechanism behind the recurring "a table missing from the ledger"
  class, now relocated from the VM (where it was mechanized) to the snapshot crate (where it
  was not), and it is why one logical small state has two encodings (F4's store half).
- **Recommendation:** a single table-of-tables (tag, image field, encode, decode, gate,
  store section, restore verb) iterated by all six sites, plus a field-count lock derived
  from `MachineImage`'s source text in the style `sidetable.rs` already uses. Unify the two
  emptiness rules on the atom rule (refuse present-but-empty) with a one-step ladder
  migration.

### F12. The CAS identity contract is not enforced on either side: `resume_from_cas` never re-hashes, and `suspend_to_cas` uses a fixed temp name in a shared directory — MEDIUM

- **File:line:** `rust/engine/ironhorse-snapshot/src/machine.rs:712-720`
  (`resume_from_cas`), `:125` (`CAS_TMP_NAME`), `:179-195` (`suspend_to_cas`). Compare
  `rust/endo/xsnap/src/lib.rs:737-755`.
- **Category:** robustness · **Confidence:** high (by inspection) · **Novelty:** new.
- **Claim:** a CAS blob whose bytes do not hash to its filename is accepted whenever it
  passes structural validation, and two machines suspending concurrently into one `cas_dir`
  both create `.snapshot.tmp`, so one `rename` can publish interleaved bytes under a digest
  they do not hash to.
- **Evidence:**
  ```rust
  const CAS_TMP_NAME: &str = ".snapshot.tmp";
  ```
  ```rust
  let path = cas_dir.join(sha256);
  let file = File::open(&path)?;
  from_snapshot_file(file, expected_sig)
  ```
  The surface this mirrors already fixed it: xsnap uses `.snapshot.{pid}.{seq}.tmp` with the
  comment "a fixed name would let two processes ... interleave writes and corrupt each
  other", and removes the temp file on failure (ironhorse does not).
- **Impact:** the CAS's only integrity property is `name == sha256(content)`; neither side
  checks it. The store path, by contrast, verifies every row against sealed leaf hashes on
  every fault. Also note `cas_dir.join(sha256)` performs no validation of the digest string,
  so a caller-supplied path component escapes the directory.
- **Recommendation:** re-hash in `from_snapshot_file`/`resume_from_cas` (the bytes are
  already fully in memory — it is free) and refuse a mismatch; use a pid+seq temp name and
  remove it on the failure path; validate that `sha256` is 64 hex characters before joining.

### F13. Two divergent, hand-maintained stored-reference traversals with no mechanical net — MEDIUM

- **File:line:** `rust/engine/ironhorse-snapshot/src/image.rs:510-536`
  (`stored_unregistered_key_id`: `live`, `stack`, `arrays`, `collections` only) vs
  `ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:8745-8820` (the persist gate's ~10-holder walk,
  "Kept in the ledger's order so a new carried row is easy to slot in").
- **Category:** verification · **Confidence:** high · **Novelty:** known-open — ledger
  **L2** (P1-4 residue, "Still recorded, not fixed"); confirmed unchanged at HEAD.
- **Claim:** the id audit misses private elements, bound-function `this_arg`/`args`,
  generator frames, disposable records, promise results and accessor rows, and neither
  traversal is derived from anything, so a table added tomorrow can be missed by both with
  nothing failing at build time.
- **Impact:** the audit's own rationale — "an unregistered id maps to nothing on resume" —
  applies to every holder equally. The wave-5 widening of the *bounds* walk to all tables
  was not mirrored here.
- **Recommendation:** express both as one visitor over a derived table-of-tables (see F11)
  so "traverse, don't enumerate" is realized once, and add the missing holders in the
  meantime.

### F14. The seam's own correctness argument is carried by doc comments that now assert the opposite of the code — LOW

- **File:line:** `rust/engine/ironhorse-vm/src/interp.rs:8599-8610` ("These two side tables
  do not travel … a resumed guest function is uncallable today"), `:8285-8286` ("only

- **Category:** docs-drift · **Confidence:** high · **Novelty:** new.
- **Claim:** the persist gate's stated rationale is false: `FUNC`/`PRXY`/`ACCS` all travel

  lens brief itself carried the "a resumed guest function is uncallable today?!" question

- **Recommendation:** treat these comments as code. The `sidetable.rs` reconciliation test

### F15. `SmallState::decode` and `peek_cost_table_version` use unchecked `i + len` where every sibling decoder uses `checked_add` — LOW

- **File:line:** `rust/engine/ironhorse-snapshot/src/store.rs:1439-1444`, `:1949-1954`;
  contrast `image.rs:716-723`.
- **Category:** robustness · **Confidence:** high · **Novelty:** known-open — ledger
  **L7**, "Recorded but not verified"; now verified.
- **Claim:** on a 32-bit `usize`, a crafted section length near `u32::MAX` wraps past the
  gate and the slice panics anonymously instead of returning a named `Corrupt`. Unreachable
  on 64-bit.
- **Evidence:**
  ```rust
  let len = u32::from_be_bytes([p[i], p[i + 1], p[i + 2], p[i + 3]]) as usize;

  if i + len > p.len() { return Err(...); }
  ```

  vs the sibling's explicit rationale: "`len` is attacker-sized (a full u32), so on a

- **Recommendation:** `i.checked_add(len)`, two sites. The branch has already fixed this
  class three times elsewhere; the discipline should be uniform or the doc claim
  ("decoders fail closed on every target") should be scoped.

## 3. Strengths

- **One gate, one decoder set, two callers.** `check_image_slot_bounds` (image.rs:3478) and
  every `decode_*` are shared verbatim by the container path and both store paths
  (store.rs:2975, :3317, image.rs:4645). The class "the store path forgot a check" is
  structurally closed, and the ledger records that this was learned the hard way.
- **Proof tokens instead of conventions, where they exist.** `ValidatedSnapshot`
  (image.rs:411-437, `pub(crate)` constructor, consumed by `image_to_interp`) and
  `ValidatedStoreState` (store.rs:3059-3092) make "validated before restore" a type
  property. F3 is a request to finish the job on the write side, not a criticism of the
  pattern.
- **The persist gate reasons in the sound direction.** Computing the doomed set and
  traversing what is written (interp.rs:8688-8730), rather than enumerating holders, is the
  correct response to the wave-6 meta-cause, and the O(dirty) checkpoint form's induction
  argument survives scrutiny: the doomed set is provably empty on a freshly resumed
  machine, and a reference can only enter a page by dirtying it.
- **Determinism hygiene is uniform and deliberate.** Every one of ~25 snapshot emitters
  sorts by owner (interp.rs:8549 … :10511); witnesses take `min`; compacted arenas are
  emitted densely; f64 travels as raw bits; page edges and reachability sets are
  `BTreeSet`s; and `metamorphic_determinism.rs` pins a golden vector precisely because
  every other comparison is self-referential within one process.
- **Fail-closed decoding on untrusted bytes is genuinely thorough.** Every
  `Vec::with_capacity` is clamped to what the payload can hold, `checked_mul`/`checked_add`
  guard the record-array gates, the atom envelope refuses trailing bytes, duplicate tags,
  unknown tags and out-of-order atoms, and I found no reachable panic on malformed input
  outside the two holes in F2 and F4.
- **Cross-table validation where all tables are in hand.** The promise cluster
  (image.rs:2365-2635, ~270 lines) and the generator-frame PC walk with nested bodies
  subtracted (image.rs:3916-4014) are model work, each rule stating the resume-time
  consequence it prevents.
- **Migration is verify-then-restamp**, with signature and cost-table gates before the first
  byte changes, a strict-advance guard, and `reread_manifest` to defeat stale handle caches
  — each with the finding that motivated it recorded inline.
- **The tests read as an audit log.** Nearly every refusal names the wave, review or fuzz
  trophy that motivated it, which is what made "is this still true?" answerable at all.

## 4. Recommended architectural direction

**Move the persist precondition from the verbs into the type system, and make quiescence a
lifecycle fact rather than a table-emptiness heuristic.** F1 and F3 are the same defect seen
from two sides: the property "this image is safe to persist" is currently asserted by three
functions that a caller may or may not go through, using a predicate that infers a
lifecycle state from nine field emptiness checks. Both halves are cheap to fix and both
fixes are mechanical. Have `run` set a `last_crank_completed` latch and test it in
`is_quiescent`; have `snapshot_image` return a token that `write_machine` and
`image_to_batch` require. That converts the seam's central safety property from "every
caller remembers" into "the compiler refuses", which is the standard this crate already
meets on the read side with `ValidatedSnapshot`.

**Finish canonicality one layer down, and make the fuzz harness able to see it.** The
branch has done the hard conceptual work — "one logical machine, one encoding, or the CAS
key is not a key" — and enforced it at the envelope and across 24 modern decoders. The six
stage-6 atoms and the store's section stream were never brought under the rule (F4), and
the invariance fuzz arm checks byte-identity only on writer-produced inputs, which is
precisely why nobody noticed. Adding `Cursor::done()`-style exact consumption to six
decoders and extending the fuzz arm to assert `write(read(b)) == b` on *accepted mutated*
bytes closes the class and keeps it closed. The same pass should unify the two emptiness
grammars (atom vs store section) so that one logical small state has one root.

**Invest the next verification effort in the instrument's blind spots rather than in more
scenarios.** The suite is the right idea — one instrument, every backend — but it cannot
observe durability (never reopens), cannot observe a refused commit (every commit is
`.expect`ed), and cannot reach a halted crank (every crank is asserted to complete). Those
three gaps cover F1, F6 and the entire trait contract; they are all additive changes to
`store_suite.rs` that require no trait change. Parameterizing the *suspend schedule* (an
arbitrary subset of boundaries, not just "all" or "none") is the single highest-yield
addition, because transparency across arbitrary suspend points is the property the whole
seam exists to provide, and it is currently sampled at three points. Alongside that, the
recurring "N tables × M hand-maintained lists" pressure (F11, F13) should be answered the
way the VM answered it for GC roots: with a derived registry, before the next graduation
adds a tenth site.
