# Problem

I’d like to discuss terminology, scoped to OCapN and Endo, to improve how we communicate ideas. Some of our traditional jargon is challenging for newcomers. Nobody’s sure what an unum is. It’s taken me five years from hearing the term sturdyref and understanding what a sturdyref is and why it exists. It’s not clear in what sense a sturdyref is sturdy or a reference. It’s not clear to me, at least, what distinguishes an offline capability from an online capability.

So, I would like to iterate with this group on a tentative glossary.

# Tentative Object Capability Glossary

> This is an exercise in attempting to establish more clear names for the
> purposes of conveying the fundamental concepts of an object capability system
> with a minimum of confusion and maximum of correct inferrence.
> It is by no means perfect or final and I share with the hope of flushing out
> better ideas, and experimentally verifying that these terms, in the few cases
> they differ from tradition, click better than our usual jargon.

- **Capability Transfer Protocol**:
  A family of protocols that, like Remote Procedure Call protocols, can invoke functions by passing messages.
  However, messages can carry and be delivered to references.
  Every request produces an immediate promise for the response, so requests can be pipelined over responses.
  Capability Transfer Protocols include the original CapTP from the E language, Cap'n Proto, Cap'n Web, and OCapN.

- **Message**:
  a request or response that can carry values.

- **Request**:
  a message that caries arguments to 

- **Delivery**:
  a function application or method invocation delivered to a reference,
  accepting request arguments and producing a promise for a response value.
  In OCapN, we differentiate method invocation from function application by convention.
  Method invications are conventionally equivalent to function application
  where the first argument is a Symbol naming the method.

## Passable Value Model

- **Value**:
  Values include Data and Capabilities that can be passed in Messages.

- **Simple Value**:
  Simple values include Null, Undefined, Boolean, Integer (range of bigint),
  Float64, ByteArray, String, and Symbol.

  Simple values are "passed by copy".

- **Data Value**:
  Simple and compound values that do not include any capabilities, transitively.

  Data values are "passed by copy".

- **Compound Value**:
  Struct, List, and Tag: values consisting of other values.

  Compound values are "passed by copy" but may close over capabilities that are
  passed indirectly.

- **Reference / Referent**:
  References are values that can respond to requests.

  References are "passed by reference".

- **Promise**:
  A value representing the eventual fulfillment Value or rejection Error in
  response to a delivery.

  Pointers are "passed by promise".
  This is mostly a pun on pass-by-promise and pass-by-reference, but there
  is a legitimate distinction.
  Chains of promises, when a promise is Resolved to another Promise, can move
  between peers, such that an originating peer can go offline but a promise can
  still make progress toward its settled Value.

- **Error**:
  Like a Compound Value, an error is pass-by-copy to the extent that it bears a
  message and an arbitrary Struct for supplementary Data, which must
  not close over any Capabilities, such that an Error can be serialized
  without any supplementary Capability Data.
  Errors are equal to the extent that their exposed message and data are
  transitively equal.
  An OCapN client may also map an Error to a corresponding error identifier,
  which should be the same for every copy of an Error regardless of its path
  through a Network, but does not participate in Pass-Invariant Equality.
  That is, Errors are equal if and only if their data are equal, irrespective
  of their identifier.

- **Capability Data (Capdata)**:
  For purposes of serialization, a Value is a tree of in-band Data values and
  place-hodlers for Capabilities (the "body"), with out-of-band metadata
  mapping each of the Capabilities to tables from the perspective of a
  particular client (the "slots").
  A virtue of separating the "body" from the "slots" is that the slots
  can be translated at the boundaries between clients without needing to
  translate or even inspect the body.

- **Handle / Handler**:
  (Reference or Promise) A value that can handle messages, either by queueing
  and forwarding them like a Promise, or responding to them like a Reference.

- **Pointer**:
  A kind of Value that refers to another value indirectly.
  Following a pointer leads to another value.
  Pointers can be passed to another Peer and do not require the sender to be online
  to produce a value on the associated peer.
  Pointers and Locators are collectively Links.

  Pointers are "passed by pointer", meaning that the corresponding Locator data
  are copied to the destination and the original peer does not need to be
  online to follow the pointer.

  > Pointers are called Sturdyrefs in E and related systems.

- **Capability**:
  (Reference, Promise, Handle, or Pointer)
  A capability represents "permission" to use something.
  So long as one holds a capability, they can exercise the capability.
  For a reference, that is the "right" to deliver requests and receive promises
  for responses to the referent.

  > When speaking about capabilities outside the context of OCapN, it is
  > sometimes useful to call Capabilities "Object Capabilities" or "OCaps" to
  > distinguish them from similar but distinct concepts in other systems.
  > Neither "Object" nor "Capability" capture or isolate the concept of an
  > "Object Capability" without help.

- **Authority**:
  Authority is the range of behavior one can exercise that emerges from the
  combination of code and capabilities.
  Code can exercise authority that is either greater or less than the sum of
  the capabilities it holds.
  For this reason, in an object capability system, we can reason that the
  authority of code in a sandbox is not necessarily exploitation of every
  capability available to the system (Amplification) and some programs can
  reliably reduce the authority (Attenuate) implied by a capability.

- **Locator**:
  A textual representation of a capability that can be carried off-the-wire or
  off-the-heap.
  A locator may be a URL, or a URL in a QR code, among other possible
  representations.
  A locator designates a peer with a verifiable public key, identifies a Value
  that peer may hold
  Locators retain a value weakly, meaning that a peer has no obligation to
  retain the value indefinitely and may reject a request for the identified
  value.

* **Identifier**:
  A large, secret number that designates a Value on a particular Peer.

  > Identifiers are alternately called Nonces or Swissnums in E.
  > The E Nonce Locator, in particular, provides Values for valid, known
  > Nonces.
  > A weakness of the term Identifier is that the word bears a very
  > different meaning in language design, and Identifier does not
  > convey unforgeability.
  > Swissnum evokes a possibly apocrophal property of Swiss Bank Accounts,
  > where simply knowing an account nubmer is sufficient for access.

- **Link**:
  (Pointer or Locator)
  A link represents a capability to get a Value, either on-the-wire,
  on-the-heap, or off of either, like a Locator written on the side of a bus.

  > Links correspond to the concept of an "Offline Capability" in E, which
  > come in the form of a Sturdyref or a Locator.

# Special capabilities

- **Greeter**:
  The first value presented by a peer in a fresh OCapN session, through which
  any other value must be obtained.

  > This is called a Bootstrap value in Endo.

- **Directory / Director**:
  For purposes of enabling Three-Party Hand-off within a Network, every Peer
  presents a Directory, which is a Protocol for getting the Value for an
  Identifier and 

  > This is called a Nonce Locator in E and has been called the Bootstrap in
  > the OCapN draft specification.

- **Projector**:
  A closely-held capability of an OCapN client that can reveal the Locator for
  a Pointer, or produce a Pointer for a Locator.

  > This is called an Introducer in E.

  > A similar capability might reveal the error identifier for an Error, for

## Core concepts

- **Network**:
  A graph of Peers within which a Reference can be passed by Three-party
  Hand-offs along multiple paths through the network and reliably be equal
  to itself on any peer these references converge upon, such that 

- **Transport**:
  A protocol that carries messages between peers.
  For example, a combination of HTTP and TLS.

- **Encoding**:
  A protocol for serializing messages, like CBOR.

- **Framing**:
  A protocol that ensures FIFO delivery of delimited chunks, like WebSocket.

- **Peer**:
  A node in a Network with a verifiable cryptographic identity and potentially
  home to References.

- **Connection Hint**:
  In the context of a particular Network and Transport,
  information needed to establish a Session.

- **Session**:

  The scope that an OCapN client is obligated to maintain tables that translate
  and retain capabilities between a pair of peers.
  When a session ends, all locally unreachable capabilities are elligible
  for collection.

  > Depending on the Transport, a Session may be scoped to a Connection, but
  > does not need to.
  > The four-day default timeout for TCP provides no indication of actual
  > liveness of a connection, and application level heartbeats do not prove
  > disconnection, so E, for example, had application layer timeouts such that
  > the necessary tables were preserved long enough that a TCP level connection
  > could restore a session.

- **Local**:
  Capabilities in the same Worker, and by implication necessarilly the same
  Peer.

- **Remote**:
  Capabilities in another Worker, possibly another Peer.

## Promise states

- **Pending**:
  The initial state of a promise, waiting to be Resolved.

- **Resolved**:
  A Resolved promise is either Settled or Forwarded.

- **Forwarded**:
  A Promise may be Resolved to another Promise, in which case all of its
  prior and subsequent messages are forwarded to the resolution.

- **Settled**:
  A Settled promise is either Fulfilled or Rejected.

- **Fulfilled**:
  Settled with a value of any Value type except a Promise.
  All messages delivered to a fulfilled promise are forwarded to the Value.

- **Rejected**:
  Settled with a reason of type Error.
  All messages delivered to a rejected promise are notified of the rejection
  Error.

## Patterns

- **Attenuation**:
  Producing a capability with reduced authority.
  For example, attenuating a readable and writable reference to
  a read-only reference.

- **Amplification**:
  Combining capabilities to increase authority.
  For example, combining a readable reference and a writable reference
  to produce a readable and writable reference.

  Amplification can also be hazard, as one can combine the capability to read a
  secret with the capability to exfiltrate said secret.

- **Revocability**:
  A common attenuation, where an authority is made temporary at the discretion
  of another party.
  The controlling party can revoke a capability and it will reject all
  subsequent requests.

- **Membrane**:
  An adapter that intercepts and may "distort" every message that passes
  through it to the capabilities behind it.
  By convention, a two-sided membrane has a "wet" and "dry" side.
  Membranes may have any number of sides and, by convention, membranes
  with three or more facets label their sides by colors like "blue", "yellow",
  and "green".

  For every reference that passes through the membrane, the membrane grows
  to have a proxy reference on the opposite side that preserves the distortion.

  The canonical distortion is to wrap all references to make them revocable.
  A revocable membrane is a reusable tool for distorting all references
  through a membrane such that the controller can cause all subsequent
  dliveries to be rejected at a time of their choosing.

  For example, making a single directory reference revocable could be trivially
  escaped by navigating to a child directory that has not been revoked yet.
  A revocable membrane around a directory reference would make all reachable
  directories revocable with a single, central revocation.

- **Delegation**:
  Passing a capability, possibly attenuated, to another party.

- **Brand**:
  A protocol design pattern that mimics the properties of public key
  signature cyrptography, standing on an object capability substrate.
  A brand is simply a reference that is reliably equal to itself regardless
  of what path it passes through a Network, due to Pass-invariant Equality,
  which emerges mechanically from Three-party Hand-off, such that one can
  establish authenticity by comparing a reference to the known brand.

- **Sealer / Unsealer**:
  A protocol design pattern that mimics the properties of public key encryption
  and decryption, standing on an object capability substrate.
  As one can create a public and private key crytographically, and information
  encrypted with either key can only be decrypted with the other, a Sealer and
  Unsealer are an entangled pair of references, whereby the Sealer can
  encapsulate a value that only the Unsealer can extract.

- **Grant Matching**:
  [The Grant Matcher Puzzle](http://www.erights.org/elib/equality/grant-matcher/)
  is an exercise that demonstrates that Pass-Invariant-Equality, as emerges
  from Three-party Hand-off, gives programs the ability to obviate
  Man-in-the-Middle attacks.

- **Confused Deputy / Comingled Authority**:
  An exploitable design hazard where multiple agents entrust a program with
  various authorities, where one agent becomes inadvertently able to exercise
  another agent's authority.

- **Three-Party Hand-off**:
  A feature of some Capability Transfer Protocols like OCapN that ensures that
  references, by default, are reachable if and only if the peer that owns the
  reference is reachable and online, and also enables Pass Invariant Equality
  within a Network of Peers.
  That is, that a value passed through a Network through multiple routes may
  converge on a Peer and all such references will be equal to each other.

- **Pass-by-proxy**:
  The properties of Three-Party Hand-off are not always desirable or achievable.
  A common attenuation of a reference is simply a proxy that forwards messages
  to a target reference, but obscures the location and identity of the
  referent, but with two prices: the proxy will not be equal to the referent or
  other proxies to the referent, and the referent will not be reachable if
  either the proxy or the referent's peers are offline or unreachable.

  This kind of attenuation can be the basis of a Man-in-the-Middle attack,
  where the attenuator can intermediate messages sent through the proxy to the
  referent.
  Three-party Hand-off and Pass-Invariant Equality gives a defender a place to
  stand to defend against this kind of attack, but giving them a tool to verify
  the equality, and therefore authenticity, of a capability.

- **Distributed Confinement**:
  A group of peers can collude to confine a guest program that is distributed
  among all the peers.
  In particular, the Locator for any Pointer is transparent on the wire among
  all participating OCapN clients, but opaque to guest programs.
  These programs can still follow Pointers to a corresponding Value, but cannot
  directly observe information in the corresponding Locator.

- **Protocol**:
  References that participate in a Protocol are expected to handle request
  messages of a particular shape or signature and produce corresponding
  response messages, and may constrain references in the request or resonse to
  participate in related protocols.
  Protocols are beyond the scope of the OCapN CapTP specification but may be
  their own coordinated specifications.

- **Pass Invariant Equality**:
  A property of OCapN made possible by Three-Party Hand-off whereby a Reference
  that passes through a Network over multiple routes that converge on a
  particular Peer will be equal to every copy of itself, for a language and
  implementation-dependent "eq" (pronounced "eek") operator.

## Processing Abstractions

- **Worker**:
  A machine with its own stack, heap, message queue, and an event loop, that
  communicates with other workers asynchronously.

- **Vat**:
  A criterion, analogous to a Turing Machine, for deadlock-free distributed,
  robust communication among programs.
  A vat has an unbounded stack, heap, and message queue, where each vat
  serially drains its message queue, proceeding to the next message only when
  the stack for the previous message is empty, where each event is "run to
  completion" and no two vats share any heap or stack memory and communicate
  with each other solely by enqueueing messages to one another without
  blocking.

  Workers generally aspire to be Vats.

## Persistence of Capabilities

- **Ephemeral**:
  Capabilities are generally ephemeral, meaning that they are permanently
  unreachable if they are ever released to a garbage collector or if the
  surrounding process terminates.

- **Virtual**:
  A virtual capability is one that can maintain its identity in a worker's heap
  but page its state in and out of off-heap storage.

- **Durable**:
  A durable capability is one that persists its state to off-heap storage such
  that it can be restored if the surrounding worker terminates and a subsequent
  worker inherits its state, either when it is restarted or its code is upgraded.

- **Restart**:
  When a worker exists and a new worker inherits its program and state.

- **Upgrade**:
  When a worker exits and a new worker inherits its state but a new program.

- **Transcript Replay**:
  Running a deterministic program again from the beginning, sending all of
  the same messages it previously received, in order to reconstruct
  its heap state from the time its Worker last terminated.

- **Resumable Heap Snapshot**:
  A file that captures all of the state in a program's heap (when the stack and
  queue are empty) such that a worker can resume from that state.

- **Manual Persisence**:
  When a program manually synchronizes its state with associated storage,
  like a database.

- **Orthogonal Persistence**:
  When a program presumes to live forever and maintains all of its state
  in a heap.

- **Automatic Persistence**:
  When a program expects to periodically lose all of tis ephemeral state due to
  a Restart or Upgrade and so uses Durable capabilities to preserve
  all important state between Incarnations.

- **Ephermeral Persistence**:
  Deterministic garbage collection of a program's heap is fickle.
  For a particular version of a runtime, it is achievable, but unreasonable to
  hold invariant between versions of a runtime.
  One theorized solution to this problem is to take advantage of resumable heap
  snapshots to rapidly restore a program to a pristine heap every time it
  finishes draining its message queue, thus making the need to track
  capabilities that are retained by the runtime heap, and thus to remove
  garbage collection variation as a concern for the determinism of a resulting
  transcript.
  
- **Manchurian Upgrade**:
  A software Upgrade that uses a full Transcript Replay to simulate the
  same behavior as a prior, recorded run, that is permitted to deviate
  from the original processes projected behavior when the transcript
  is exhausted.
  A program that does not leave sufficient Durable capabilities to
  perform an ordinary Upgrade may elect to perform a Manchurian Upgrade,
  where the only deviation from the original behavior is to create
  Durable state for a subsequent ordinary Restart or Upgrade.

- **Béjar-Hoffman Box**:
  A theoretical language feature analogous to a JavaScript FinalizationRegistry,
  such that a CapTP client can monitor whether a reference it retains on
  a remote Peer remains reachable from remote references to local references.
  Béjar Boxes would make garbage collection of distributed cycles possible.

  > I have taken the liberty to name this feature after Arturo Béjar, who
  > Mark S. Miller and Chip Morningstar credit for theorizing this mechanism
  > in the 90s, but leaving no discernable record of the theory,
  > and after Mathieu Hoffman who independently proposed the same theory in his
  > interview to join Agoric around 2020.
